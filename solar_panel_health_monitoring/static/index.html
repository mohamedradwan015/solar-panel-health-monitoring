Checking model

What the model does

Each panel reading (voltage and current) is compared with 153,834 readings from normal operation on four BIRDS satellites. The score is the mean distance to the 20 most similar normal readings. A reading is flagged when its score is above a fixed threshold.

The threshold is the 99.5th percentile of out-of-session reference scores, so about 0.5 % of healthy readings are flagged by design. The score is a distance, not a probability, and the flagged share is not a damage percentage.

What it cannot do

  • It does not confirm physical faults or locate failed cells.
  • Subtle faults (partial degradation, stuck cells, intermittent bursts) are mostly missed.
  • Its evidence is synthetic or rule-derived; no verified real fault labels were available.
  • The baseline expects BIRDS-like voltage and current scales. Other hardware may need a new baseline, and the app refuses data outside that scale.

Accepted layouts

  • BIRDS workbook: one sheet per session with Time stamp, Vpy (mV), Ipy (mA) columns.
  • Flat file: elapsed_seconds, P1_voltage_V, P1_current_A ... one column pair per panel.
  • Long file: one reading per row with panel_id, time, voltage and current columns.

Any number of panels is accepted. Units are read from the column names and converted once; they are never guessed.

Column mapping (advanced)

Only for files with unusual headers. A JSON object keyed by column name, for example {"ts":"time","col_a":{"measure":"voltage","channel":"PANEL_A","unit":"mV"}}.

How to read these results

Model outputs

Score, threshold and the flag come straight from the supplied model. Diagnosis labels (for example suspected low output) are the model package's cautious wording and never confirm a fault.

Derived by this app

Counts, percentages, intervals (consecutive flagged readings) and the status badge are arithmetic on those outputs. The badge is a convenience: Within expected alert rate means at most 2 % flagged and no persistent run, High alert rate means 10 % or more, and Review is everything between. It is not a verified fault class.

Before you rely on it

The detector's measured performance comes from synthetic and rule-derived tests. Treat flags as a prompt to look at the telemetry, not as a diagnosis.
